# Company definitions and decision review

A company's brain should preserve what business terms mean and why work was
accepted. Keep definitions local and evidenced. "Delivered" might mean merged
code in an engineering dashboard while a customer commitment requires deployed
functionality and customer acceptance. Use the definition applicable to the
question, record and company rather than collapsing those meanings.

## Minimal reasoning chain

```text
Original passage → scoped claim → assumption/interpretation → decision
                                                        → commitment → work
New evidence → challenges assumption → dependent decision/commitment needs review
```

Describe the relationship on the link: supports, reports, challenges, depends on,
or context only. Preserve the source's observed/reported/inferred basis when it
matters. This describes what the source does; it does not certify its validity.

A customer says it will sign if a feature is accepted by December 1. A company
may decide to accept that commitment, assuming an integration finishes in time.
These are distinct records and steps, with separate evidence and authority.
The [decision template](../templates/decision.md) captures that small chain;
[definitions](../templates/definition.md) preserve company-specific meanings.
They are optional scaffolding. Existing topic sections and links can do the same
job; do not impose these filenames or manufacture approval to fill a table.

## Review when evidence changes

1. Inspect the new original passage and the prior evidence/estimate. State what
   changed, which company/process it covers, and what remains an estimate.
2. Find the assumption it actually challenges, then follow dependents to the
   decision, commitment and work. Follow context-only links only when they explain
   a relevant exclusion; they do not prove impact.
3. Apply the appropriate company definition and compute dates or quantities
   explicitly. A forecast beyond an approved deadline is a risk, not proof of
   a future failure or proof that the deadline has been amended.
4. Preserve original approval, dates, assumptions and source captures. Append a
   reasoned review with new evidence and unresolved choices. Keep unrelated work
   and another company's decisions unchanged.
5. Explain what needs review, why, by whom according to the recorded authority,
   and what evidence/decision could settle it. An authorized local update does
   not authorize external communication, a new approval or publication.

## Recover work in a later session

A new agent should be able to locate the decision through navigation, inspect the
original basis and dependent work, and explain the current open question. An
answer saved by an earlier agent is secondary interpretation. A session handoff
should link the relevant records, describe actual completed changes and outstanding
choices, and distinguish agent authorship from business evidence. A human-friendly
note and ordinary links are sufficient; conversation traces are supplementary.
